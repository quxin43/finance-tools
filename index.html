Dip-buying ladder

Starting when the price reaches s% of its peak, and every further d% after that, you add a tranche: I, then I×m, then I×m², and so on. Each tranche uses leverage L. The price bottoms at dmin below the peak and then rebounds. The chart shows your total gain or loss at each rebound level.

Assumes the price falls straight to the bottom and then rises, each tranche is margined on its own (total units, total margin and average price count only tranches not yet liquidated at that point; margin is the invested amount valued at the current price, invested × price ÷ buy price, so it shrinks in step with the price; P&L still includes the leveraged part of the position), and there are no fees, funding costs or interest. With liquidation off, a leveraged tranche can lose more than you put in.

Rally ladder (short selling)

The mirror of the dip ladder. Starting when the price reaches s% of its bottom, and every further d% after that, you sell short a tranche: I, then I×m, then I×m², and so on. Each tranche uses leverage L. The price tops out at dmax above the bottom and then falls. The chart shows your total gain or loss at each drop level.

Ratio trade: A against B

Today the price ratio A/B is pri. You expect it to end somewhere between a best case prfb and a worst case prfw. If prfb is below pri the trade is short A, long B; if it is above, long A, short B. The chart shows your gain for every split of 10,000 between A and B, for several final prices of A (pA′/pA). For each one, B's final price follows from the final ratio: pB′/pB = (pA′/pA) × pri / prf.

Front-month futures prices, all converted to USD per barrel so each pair is comparable (gallons × 42, tonnes of gasoil ÷ 7.45). The gap is the first price minus the second. The gap line is black; days more than 2σ from the period average are marked with pink points, and more than 3σ with red points.

Energy market hours in Singapore time

Hover a bar for exact times. The white markers show each market's daily settlement, which sets the price used here. NYMEX pauses daily 17:00–18:00 New York; ICE pauses 23:00–01:00 London. The energy update runs inside the NYMEX break: 05:15 SGT when the US is on summer time, 06:15 SGT in winter. Times move with US/UK daylight saving.

Metal ratio analysis

Front-month futures in USD per troy ounce (copper in USD per pound). Each ratio is the first price divided by the second: gold ÷ silver is how many ounces of silver one ounce of gold buys; a high ratio means the second metal is cheap compared with the first. Gold ÷ platinum and platinum ÷ palladium are relative-value pairs (platinum and palladium substitute for each other in car catalysts). Copper ÷ gold (× 1,000) is a growth-versus-safety gauge: it tends to rise when markets expect stronger growth and to move with the US 10-year bond yield. The ratio line is black; days more than 2σ from the period average are marked with pink points, and more than 3σ with red points.

Agriculture gap analysis

Front-month CBOT futures. Grains are in US cents per bushel, as quoted by the exchange and CMC. BOHO (soybean oil − heating oil, USD per gallon) is the biodiesel margin gauge. Soybeans ÷ corn drives US planting: farmers switch acres to whichever pays more (for new-crop November soybeans ÷ December corn, about 2.4 or above favours soybeans, about 2.2 or below favours corn). Wheat − corn is a feed-substitution spread: when wheat gets close to corn, it is fed to livestock instead. The soybean crush is the processors' margin: oil and meal from one bushel minus the bushel's price. Each panel can show the gap or the ratio; the line is black, with pink points beyond 2σ and red points beyond 3σ of the period average.
